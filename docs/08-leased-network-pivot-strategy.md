# Hit House — Leased Network Pivot Strategy

**Company:** Hasenpfeffer Ventures LLC (operating as **Hit House**) · **Model:** Asset-light, lease-led, multi-location creator membership network
**Document type:** Strategic pivot — from a single owned vertical building to interchangeable leased locations
**Version:** 3.0 · **Horizon:** 5 years · **Supersedes:** the single-flagship premise in docs 01, 02, and 07 wherever they conflict
**Prepared for:** Founders, the board, operators, and prospective capital partners

> **Why this document exists.** The property at 5 East 47th Street is no longer available. The prior plan made that one four-story building the entire business — the brand was the address, the moat was *"the Address,"* and capital was raised to buy and build out floors. This document pivots the company to a **lease-led, location-interchangeable** model and renames the project **Hit House**. Where the canvas (01), business plan (02), and SPV strategy (07) still describe a single owned building, **this document governs.**

> **Terminology note.** Operating vocabulary as elsewhere in `/docs` (Production Cycle, Project Slate, Confidentiality & Security Protocol). The legal entity remains **Hasenpfeffer Ventures LLC**; **Hit House** is the brand. All figures are illustrative planning estimates anchored to the unchanged operating constants: 100 residents per Production Cycle, 4 cycles/year, $3,500 non-refundable entry, the 70/20/10 Project Participation split, and monthly residency dues during the 90-day cycle.

---

## 1. What changed, and why it's an upgrade

The old thesis bet everything on one irreplaceable address. That was the moat *and* the single point of failure — and it just failed: the building is gone before a lease was signed. Rebuilding the same bet on a different building would re-create the same fragility and the same heavy, slow, capital-intensive entry.

So the pivot is not "find another building." It is **"stop making the building the product."** Hit House keeps everything that was actually valuable — the invitation-only cohort, the Production Cycle, the Confidentiality & Security Protocol, the compounding Project Slate IP, and Sam — and changes only *where* it happens: in **leased, interchangeable spaces** the company can stand up, swap, and scale across cities.

This is strictly better on the dimensions that matter for a young network:

- **Lower capital intensity.** No purchase, no multimillion-dollar four-floor buildout. Capital goes to the platform, light fit-out, lease deposits, and runway.
- **Faster and reversible market entry.** A leased House-Standard location can open in months and close at lease-end if a market underperforms — no stranded building.
- **Geographic optionality.** The cohort can be placed where talent and sponsor demand actually are this cycle, not where we happen to own concrete.
- **Replication is the model, not Phase II.** Multi-location was previously a future ambition; now it is the operating reality from day one.

## 2. The new thesis — *the House is wherever the cohort is*

Hit House is an **asset-light, lease-led membership network** for the top decile of working creators. The membership, the cohort, the services, and the IP are the product. **Space is a sourced, interchangeable input** — leased to a consistent standard, fit out lightly, and run for as long as it serves the network.

The investor thesis is unchanged in substance and stronger in form: *culture has an infrastructure problem.* Hit House is still that infrastructure — it is simply **portable infrastructure**. The defensibility moves from owning one expensive address to owning **the system that can stand up a House-Standard location in any city faster, cheaper, and more discreetly than anyone else**, on top of a compounding IP catalog that travels with the network rather than the walls.

| | Old (single owned building) | New (Hit House, lease-led) |
|---|---|---|
| The product | The four-floor building | The membership, cohort, services, and Project Slate |
| Space | Owned/long-leased, fixed | Leased, interchangeable, swappable |
| Moat | "The Address" | The location-sourcing + operating system + the IP catalog |
| Capital | Buy + build out floors | Platform + deposits + light fit-out + runway |
| Expansion | Phase II, future | The operating model, day one |
| Key liability | Mortgage / buildout | Lease obligations (laddered, flexible) |

## 3. The House Standard — a portable venue specification

The old plan hard-coded four physical floors. Hit House abstracts those into the **House Standard**: a portable specification of **four functional zones** that any candidate space must provide or be lightly fit out to provide. This is what makes locations interchangeable — a space qualifies if it can host the zones, regardless of its address or layout.

| Zone | Function (was) | What a leased space must provide |
|---|---|---|
| **Zone A — Sound** | Floor 7, Recording Studios | Acoustically isolatable rooms for recording; power and quiet for portable booths and mobile recording rigs |
| **Zone B — Production & AI** | Floor 6, Post-Production & AI | Edit/post space, secure network drop to the central compute core, room for portable on-site inference rigs and a small virtual-production setup |
| **Zone C — Salon** | Floor 5, Lounge & Member Salon | A hospitality-grade convening space for receptions, listening sessions, and member lockers |
| **Zone D — Deal Floor** | Floor 4, Business & Deal Floor | Private meeting rooms, secure deal/IP-admin space, sponsor presentation room |

The House Standard also fixes the **non-physical** requirements that travel to every location unchanged: the Confidentiality & Security Protocol (no-capture, private-entry, Mutual NDA, Closed-Set Policy), data-sovereignty handling, brand and hospitality standards, and Sam as the single operating surface. A location is "House-Standard" only when it meets the zone spec *and* the protocol spec.

## 4. The Location Sourcing Engine

The single most important new capability is **finding and securing the right leased spaces, continuously and ahead of demand.** This is a standing function, not a one-time real-estate search, and it runs as a pipeline:

1. **Scout** — maintain a live pipeline of candidate spaces across target and opportunistic cities (flexible/managed offices, pre-fit creative and studio spaces, hospitality conversions, short-to-medium-term commercial leases).
2. **Score** — evaluate each candidate against the House Standard (§3): zone fit, fit-out cost and lead time, lease flexibility, location quality (talent/sponsor proximity, discretion), and total cost per cycle.
3. **Secure** — negotiate and sign leases favoring flexibility: short-to-medium terms, break options, turnkey/managed terms where available, and percentage-rent or revenue-share structures where landlords will engage.
4. **Fit out (light)** — bring the space to House Standard with portable and demountable kit (mobile recording rigs, portable inference rigs, demountable acoustic treatment, furniture, brand dressing) rather than heavy construction.
5. **Operate** — run cycles in the space under Sam; the location is just another tenant of the operating system.
6. **Wind down** — at lease-end or underperformance, restore (make-good) and exit; redeploy portable kit to the next location.

This engine is what turns "the property fell through" from an existential event into a routine operation. The pipeline is deliberately kept **ahead of demand** so a cycle can always be placed in the right city at the right time, and so no single landlord or lease can hold the network hostage.

## 5. The lease-led venue model

The chosen model is **lease-led**: Hit House controls its spaces under lease and fits them lightly to the House Standard, rather than owning them or running purely on per-event partner venues. The deal types, in rough order of preference for a young network:

| Deal type | When to use | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexible / managed-office or turnkey creative lease** | New or unproven markets; first cycle in a city | Higher per-month cost, but minimal fit-out, fast open, easy exit |
| **Short-to-medium conventional lease (1–3 yr, with breaks)** | Markets with proven selection pressure | Lower per-month cost; deposit + light fit-out + make-good obligation |
| **Percentage-rent / revenue-share** | Landlords willing to share upside; hospitality conversions | Aligns rent with cycle performance; caps downside |
| **Pop-up / per-event** *(supplementary only)* | Bridging between leases; testing a new city | Lowest commitment; not a primary home for a cycle |

Principle: **align lease term and flexibility to cycle cadence.** Because cohorts run in 90-day cycles, the network favors leases it can ladder, break, or convert as demand moves — never a commitment that outlives the demand that justified it.

## 6. The venue-agnostic Production Cycle

The Production Cycle is unchanged in its constants and discipline — **100 residents, 4 cycles/year, $3,500 non-refundable entry, 90 days, 24-hour acceptance, monthly dues, 40/30/20/10 mix** — but it is now **venue-agnostic**: it runs in any House-Standard leased space. Two consequences:

- **Concurrent multi-city cycles.** Because cycles are no longer bound to one building, Hit House can run cycles in several leased cities at once. Scaling is *more leased House-Standard locations × cycles*, not one ever-larger building.
- **Placement is a decision, not a constraint.** Each cycle is placed in the city where selection pressure and sponsor demand are highest, informed by Sam's predictive layer and the Scout pipeline.

Scarcity is still enforced per cohort (100 seats), so interchangeable locations expand the *network*, never the individual cohort.

## 7. AI Studio without a building

The old AI Studio assumed quarantined on-prem GPU arrays bolted into Floor 6 — incompatible with leased, interchangeable spaces. The pivot:

- **Central compute core.** A single colocation/cloud GPU core (one SPV — see §8 and doc 07) serves every leased location remotely over secure private links. One core amortizes across all markets instead of a buildout per building.
- **Portable on-site inference rigs.** For the data-sovereignty requirement (resident weights and outputs never leaking to third-party APIs), each location carries portable inference rigs for sensitive workloads; the heavy training/fine-tuning runs in the private central core.
- **Same promise, lighter footprint.** Residents still direct domain agents (Music, Video, 3D, Animation) through Sam; provenance still logs to the IP Catalog; sovereignty is preserved by private tenancy plus on-site rigs. The economics move from **capex per building** to **opex billed per location** via the AI-Studio-as-a-Service intercompany arrangement.

## 8. Economics & capital implications

The headline financial change is **real estate moves from capex/ownership to opex (lease).**

- **Out:** building purchase, the ~$0.5M–$2.5M four-floor buildout, and the per-building AI Studio capex tiers.
- **In:** rent, lease deposits, light/portable fit-out, make-good/restoration reserves, and a shared central-compute cost — most of it opex.
- **Net:** materially lower capital intensity and faster scaling, at the cost of **lease obligations becoming the principal liability** and per-cycle break-even depending on lease terms (hence the flexibility bias in §5).

**SPV structure (governs doc 07).** The entity map changes:

| Old SPV | New | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **NY PropCo** (owns the building) | **Per-market Leasehold / OpCo SPVs** | Each holds one market's lease(s) and runs its cycles; ring-fences lease liability per city so a bad lease can't cross markets |
| **AI Studio Equipment SPV** (per-building GPUs) | **Central Compute SPV** (one colo/cloud core + portable rigs) | One core serves all locations; capex→opex; billed per location |
| HoldCo, **Project Slate Finance SPV**, **Sponsor Royalty Pool SPV** | Unchanged | Brand/Sam/Scout/IP stay at HoldCo; Slate and Sponsor vehicles unaffected |

Each Leasehold SPV is funded **mostly with equity plus a working-capital line** — no mortgage. Its key obligations are the lease deposit, light fit-out, and make-good. The **use of funds** shifts from "four-floor buildout & equipment" to **platform (Sam + Scout) + lease working capital across launch markets + the central compute core + 18-month runway** — a smaller, more flexible inaugural raise. See doc 07 for the full revised structure, capital stacks, and document checklist.

## 9. Risks specific to a lease-led network

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Lease cost / concentration in a market | Ladder short-to-medium leases with break options; favor flexible/managed terms; ring-fence per market in its own Leasehold SPV |
| Landlord / renewal / eviction risk | Keep the Scout pipeline ahead of demand so any location is replaceable; never depend on one landlord |
| Fit-out lead time delays a cycle | Portable, demountable kit and turnkey leases; pre-qualified backup spaces per target city |
| Brand inconsistency across interchangeable spaces | The House Standard (zones + protocol + hospitality spec) is enforced before a space is cycle-ready; Sam standardizes the experience |
| Multi-city operating complexity | Sam + Scout absorb ops load; each market is its own ring-fenced SPV with standardized playbooks |
| Data sovereignty without on-prem building | Private central compute tenancy + portable on-site inference rigs for sensitive workloads |
| Make-good / restoration liabilities at exit | Reserve for restoration at lease signing; prefer spaces needing minimal reinstatement |

## 10. The agent overlay — Scout joins Sam's subagents

The pivot adds one core capability to the multi-agent system: **location sourcing and lease lifecycle.**

- **Scout (Location Agent)** — maintains the candidate-space pipeline, scores spaces against the House Standard, supports lease negotiation, tracks fit-out and make-good, and feeds placement recommendations to Sam's predictive layer. New subagent.
- **Operations / House Agent** — extended to cover multi-location venue operations and lease administration across cities.
- Every other subagent (Membership, Booking, Predictive, Finance, Access, Sponsor, Programming, Growth, IP Catalog, and the AI Studio domain agents) carries over unchanged, now operating across interchangeable locations rather than four fixed floors.

Sam remains the single orchestrator; Scout is what lets Sam answer *"where does the next cycle live?"* as routinely as it answers *"who's in it?"*
